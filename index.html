Our Patroness

Who Was St. Clare of Assisi?

St. Clare of Assisi (1194–1253) was born into a noble Italian family but gave up everything to follow Christ in radical poverty. Inspired by the preaching of St. Francis of Assisi, she fled her father's house on the night of Palm Sunday and consecrated herself to God. She founded the Order of Poor Ladies — now known worldwide as the Poor Clares.

Clare's name in Latin, Clara, means "clear" or "bright." It is a fitting name for a woman whose life radiated the light of Christ. When Saracen mercenaries threatened to overrun the monastery at San Damiano, Clare — though gravely ill — had herself carried to the wall, where she raised the Blessed Sacrament in a monstrance. The soldiers, struck by terror at the sight, fled.

It is no coincidence that a congregation of Poor Clares should have chosen candle-making and altar-bread baking as their apostolate. St. Clare raised the Blessed Sacrament against the enemies of God — and today her daughters raise candles to light the altars where that same Sacrament is confected.

More Than a Business

The Human and Spiritual Impact

When St. Clare Candle and Altar Bread Enterprise was established, it was not with the ambition of becoming a large commercial enterprise. It was with a much simpler goal: to meet the pastoral needs of the Church, and in doing so, to create dignified work for women and youth in the Ijebu-Ode area.

Every candle poured is a small source of income for a family. Every host baked is a small act of service to a priest who will, at the altar, transform that humble bread into the Body of Christ.
