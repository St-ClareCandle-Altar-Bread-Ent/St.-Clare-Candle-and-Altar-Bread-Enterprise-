Our Patroness

Who Was St. Clare of Assisi?

St. Clare of Assisi (1194–1253) was born into a noble Italian family but gave up everything to follow Christ in radical poverty. Inspired by the preaching of St. Francis of Assisi, she fled her father's house on the night of Palm Sunday and consecrated herself to God. She founded the Order of Poor Ladies — now known worldwide as the Poor Clares — a contemplative community of women who live by the rule of poverty, chastity, and obedience, dedicating their days to prayer, adoration, and manual labor.

Clare's name in Latin, Clara, means "clear" or "bright." It is a fitting name for a woman whose life radiated the light of Christ. According to one of the most beloved legends of her life, when Saracen mercenaries threatened to overrun the monastery at San Damiano, Clare — though gravely ill — had herself carried to the wall, where she raised the Blessed Sacrament in a monstrance. The soldiers, struck by terror at the sight, fled. The Church remembers her, too, as the first woman to write a monastic rule, a document of stunning spiritual clarity that continues to guide Poor Clare communities today.

It is no coincidence that a congregation of Poor Clares should have chosen candle-making and altar-bread baking as their apostolate. The connection is poetic. St. Clare raised the Blessed Sacrament against the enemies of God — and today her daughters raise candles to light the altars where that same Sacrament is confected. She wrote a rule that burned with clarity — and her daughters now burn wax to illumine sanctuaries across Nigeria.

Every candle we sell, therefore, is not merely a product. It is a small act of homage to a woman who taught the world that poverty, prayer, and light belong together. Every host we bake is a renewal of her trust that God uses the smallest things — a wafer, a flame, a quiet act of obedience — to accomplish great things in the world.

The St. Clare Difference

Why Parishes, Schools, and Families Choose Us

🕊️ Purity You Can Trust

Our altar bread is baked from only two ingredients — wheat flour and water — because the validity of the Sacrament depends on it. No preservatives, no additives, no shortcuts. Our candles use clean-burning waxes and cotton wicks, free from the harsh chemicals that spoil the air of a sanctuary.

⏳ Longevity — A Physics of Steady Light

A candle, like all matter, is governed by physics: it burns at the rate its wax and wick allow. By carefully controlling wick gauge, wax density, and cooling time, we ensure each candle burns long and evenly — never tunneling, never spluttering. This is why a St. Clare candle simply lasts longer than an ordinary one.

🔬 Made with Scientific Precision

Baking altar bread is a biological process. Wheat starch and gluten bind under heat; moisture must be driven off just enough to preserve but not so much that the host becomes brittle. Our Sisters have mastered these proportions across thousands of batches — a discipline no factory assembly line can replicate.

🙏 Made with Prayer

Every batch of hosts and every mould of candles is produced in an atmosphere of silence, Scripture, and intercession. The work itself is a form of prayer. When you receive our products, you receive the fruit of the contemplative life — not merely the output of a workshop.

💰 Affordable Monastery Prices

Because our overhead is small and our motive is service, our prices are consistently lower than commercial religious-goods suppliers — often dramatically so for bulk orders. Parishes, schools, and retreat centres can order with confidence knowing that every naira supports the monastery's works of mercy.

🚚 Reliable Delivery Across Nigeria

We ship from Ijebu-Ode to parishes and religious communities throughout Nigeria. For large orders, we can arrange direct delivery. For urgent needs (a Sunday Mass, a retreat, a feast day), WhatsApp us and we will do everything possible to meet your timeline.

More Than a Business

The Human and Spiritual Impact

When St. Clare Monastery Productions was established, it was not with the ambition of becoming a large commercial enterprise. It was with a much simpler goal: to meet the pastoral needs of the Church, and in doing so, to create dignified work for women and youth in the Ijebu-Ode area while supporting the charitable apostolates of the monastery.

Every candle poured is a small source of income for a family. Every host baked is a small act of service to a priest who will, at the altar, transform that humble bread into the Body of Christ. This is the quiet economics of grace — a business that measures its success not in naira alone, but in the number of sanctuaries lit, Masses served, and children fed through the monastery's outreach.
