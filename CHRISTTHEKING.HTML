The King Reigns

A Day of Adoration and Praise

On the final Sunday of the liturgical year, the Church crowns Christ as King of the Universe. At St. Clare Monastery Nigeria, the Sisters gathered with the faithful to celebrate the Solemnity of Our Lord Jesus Christ, King of the Universe — a day of adoration, of praise, and of quiet joy before the Blessed Sacrament.

The chapel was filled with voices raised in prayer. The Word of God was proclaimed. And at the heart of it all, the King who reigns from the altar was adored.

Reflection

What This Solemnity Means

Christ the King is the final Sunday of the liturgical year — the Sunday on which the Church gathers up all her prayers, all her readings, all her feasts, and lays them before one truth: Christ is King. Not a king of power as the world understands it, but a King of mercy, a King of the Eucharist, a King who reigns from the Cross and from the altar.

When Pope Pius XI instituted this feast in 1925, he did so in a world that was forgetting that Christ is King. A century later, the world still forgets. But in every monastery, in every parish, in every faithful heart, the Church still cries out: Christus vincit, Christus regnat, Christus imperat. Christ conquers. Christ reigns. Christ commands.

The Sisters of St. Clare Monastery Nigeria pray for the reign of Christ in Nigeria, in the Church, and in every heart. May He be King of our families, King of our parishes, King of our nation, and King of every soul who calls upon His name.
